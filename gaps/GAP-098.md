# GAP-098 — peeled to the bottom, the native bails do NOT converge

**Status:** OPEN — CONFIRMED from a second direction 2026-08-09 · **Filed:** 2026-08-09

## 2026-08-09 — a second, independent reading agrees

This file's finding was reached by PEELING 22 programs. A different instrument
now agrees: sweeping every non-`compile_fail` program under `examples/**` and
taking the FIRST bail cause gives **89 bails across 43 distinct causes, largest
bucket 8** (`direct link — linked module object(s) call the lua runtime`), then
7, then 5, then a flat tail of 18 causes with one program each.

These are two different measurements and neither substitutes for the other —
first causes are check order, which is exactly what this file and gap[095] warn
against ranking work by. What makes them worth putting side by side is that they
fail the SAME hypothesis from opposite ends. Peeling says the bottoms do not
converge; the unpeeled sweep says even the surface does not converge. A
convergent worklist would have to look flat at the bottom and *concentrated* at
the top, and it is flat at both.

One prediction in the neighbourhood was confirmed outright: gap[086]'s top row,
`global-decl-module-path` at 12 programs, is now **zero**, and the seven
`pass16_*_proof` programs it named surface `runtime-global:string` underneath.
That is a peel that actually happened in the tree, and the layer below was
exactly the one gap[095] said was hiding.

**Supersedes the working assumption** that native coverage is blocked by a small
number of substrate problems.

## Why this was measured

`scripts/native_census.duo` now records that **bail causes are LAYERED**: the
precheck reports the FIRST refusal and stops, so a ranking of first-reported
causes measures check order rather than work. Two agents were dispatched off
such a ranking and both found it wrong.

The rule that replaced it is *peel*: fix the reported cause, re-measure, record
what appears underneath. **Nobody had peeled anything**, so the depth of the
stack — and therefore the size of the worklist — was unknown.

## The finding

The hypothesis this tests — that 58-61 bails are "a small number of substrate
problems rather than 58 unrelated missing opcodes" — **is not supported by the
peel.** Nothing collapses into two or three causes. The largest bucket is two.

So 100% native coverage is closer to *dozens of independent repairs* than to a
handful, and any plan that assumes otherwise is sized wrong. That is the useful
half of a negative result.

The encouraging half: **two programs reached NATIVE after peel**, meaning their
entire barrier was surface-level and nothing in the backend was missing.

## The instrument created part of the reading, and it is excluded

Six programs bottomed at `runtime-global-call:tostring` — because **the peel
script substituted `tostring(` for `string.*` calls, and `tostring` is itself a
runtime global.** The measurement introduced the cause it then reported.

Excluded from the table above. Recorded because it is the same class as the
borrowed-tag defect found in the `ret-type:any` cluster: *a measurement that
perturbs what it measures reports the perturbation.* A peel that REPAIRS rather
than NEUTRALIZES would not have this flaw and would be the better instrument.

## Limits of this measurement, stated

- Covers `examples/*.duo` only, **not subdirectories** — 22 programs against the
  census's 61. The distribution may differ in the remainder.
- The substitutions are crude (`os.clock()` → `0.0`, `string.*` → `tostring`).
  They neutralize a call rather than repairing it, so a bottom reached this way
  is a *lower bound* on the real stack, not the real stack.
- Peel depth was capped at 5 layers. No program hit the cap, but a deeper one
  would have been recorded at layer 5 rather than at its bottom.

## What would make it authoritative

A peel that applies the CANONICAL repair at each layer — thread a world rather
than stub the clock, add the descriptor rather than delete the binding — and
runs over the full census denominator. Then the bottom distribution is the
worklist, and its shape decides whether native coverage is weeks or months.
