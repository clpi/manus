1:# GAP-111 — CHAIN-PREFER has nothing to chain: the canonical call form fails where the denied one works
2:
3:**Status:** PARTIAL (2026-08-20) — sema admits canonical `xs:map(f)` via foreign-home subject-first resolution (`gate/gap-111-subject-first.sh`); postfix chains and `any`-parameter fusion remain open · **Filed:** 2026-08-09
4:**Law:** CHAIN-PREFER · CHAIN-BREAK (owner ruling 2026-08-09) · FACE-CALL (§0.5)
5:· §1 DENY LIST
6:
7:## The ruling
8:
9:> **CHAIN-PREFER**: when a value is produced only to become the semantic subject
10:> of exactly one following relation, canonical source forms a postfix `:` chain
11:> and removes the temporary binding.
12:>
13:> Postfix `:` is the only chain operator. Leading `:` is ambient-subject
14:> invocation, not implicit pipeline continuation.
15:
16:with the canonical example:
17:
18:```duo
19:users
20:    :map(.score)
21:    :take(n)
22:    :filter(positive)
23:```
24:
25:## What works — the mechanism
26:
27:```duo
28:r = n:double():bump()          -> 11
29:r = n                          -> 11
30:    :double()
31:    :bump()
32:```
33:
34:**Postfix chaining works, one-line and multiline offside.** The chain operator
35:is implemented and the layout rule holds. That is the good half.
36:
37:## What fails — everything it would chain
38:
39:```
40:std.iter.map(xs, twice)     operation-first, on the §1 DENY LIST     ->  2
41:xs:map(twice)               subject-first, CANONICAL                 ->  type error
42:xs:take(2)                                                           ->  type error
43:xs:map(.s)                  field shorthand                          ->  no output
44:```
45:
46:`map`, `filter` and `take` DO exist — in `lib/iter.id`, declared
47:operation-first with every parameter `any`:
48:
49:```duo
50:map: any = (list: any, fn_ref: any)
51:filter: any = (list: any, fn_ref: any)
52:take: any = (list: any, n: i64)
53:```
54:
55:So the pipeline vocabulary is reachable only through the spelling FACE-CALL
56:denies (`std.iter.map(xs, f)`), and unreachable through the spelling FACE-CALL
57:requires (`xs:map(f)`).
58:
59:**CHAIN-PREFER cannot be canonical because the chain has nothing chainable.**
60:The canonical example in the ruling does not compile.
61:
62:## This is the fourth instance of one pattern
63:
64:| consolidation | works | canonical form | state |
65:|---|---|---|---|
66:| ambient sigil (gap[107]) | legacy prefix `@c.emit` | `@{…}`, `x@rel` | unparsed / a synonym for `.` |
67:| application (gap[110]) | `point{ x, y }` | `p: point = { x, y }` | breaks on read |
68:| ambient fields (gap[107]) | `.x` | bare `x` in a descriptor home | undeclared identifier |
69:| **pipeline (here)** | `std.iter.map(xs, f)` | `xs:map(f)` | type error |
70:
71:**Across every consolidation the law asks for, the explicit or denied form works
72:and the canonical or inferred form does not.** That is not four bugs; it is one
73:shape, and it says the rulings describe the language's target rather than its
74:present.
75:
76:Worth stating because it changes how the rulings should be read by anyone
77:implementing them: each is a specification of work, not a description of
78:behaviour, and a doc or website example written from the ruling will not run.
79:
80:## Order
81:
82:1. **`xs:map(f)` before CHAIN-PREFER.** A canonicalizer that rewrites temporaries
83:   into chains would produce source that does not compile. The subject-first
84:   reach must work first.
85:2. **The `any` parameters are the same blocker one level down.** `map(list: any,
86:   fn_ref: any)` forfeits every descriptor fact, so even when the call form
87:   works, fusion has nothing to fuse on and `xs:map(f):take(n)` cannot stream —
88:   which is the whole point of preferring the chain.
89:3. **Then CHAIN-PREFER as a canonicalizer rule**, with CHAIN-BREAK's four
90:   exceptions (multiple consumers, semantic name, effect/world boundary,
91:   operations targeting the original ambient subject) as its guard.
92:
93:## Do not
94:
95:**Do not implement CHAIN-PREFER as a formatter rewrite first.** It would delete
96:temporaries and produce chains that fail to compile, and the failure would look
97:like a formatter bug rather than a missing relation surface.
98:
99:**Do not add `map`/`filter`/`take` as new native primitives on `str` or table.**
100:gap[106] records why: they belong to a SEQUENCE basis
101:(`at`/`iter`/`put`/`len`) and hand-writing them per descriptor is the thing the
102:basis measurement exists to delete.
103:
104:## Evidence

- `gate/gap-111-map-ambiguity.sh` — `xs:map(f)` refused with explicit ambiguity while `map` exists in both `iter` and `table`.
- `gate/architecture-companion.sh` — runs both surface + ambiguity companions.
- `gate/architecture-negative.sh` — pinned debt ratchets for DNIR AST operand
  filtering, name-based record inference, and home-priority foreign dispatch

  zero downstream reconstruction.
