## The finding

`NodeKind` and `EdgeKind` violate the specification on **four** counts at once,
and they are not alone:

| law | what it says | what `NodeKind` is |
|---|---|---|
| §0.1 LAW-ONE | "No uppercase EVER" | two capitals |
| §0.3 INFERRED CASES | case-sets INLINE at the field; **"NO companion `*_kind`/`*_type` descriptors"** | a companion `*Kind` descriptor, exactly |
| `law.arch.relation` | a distinction recoverable from operands may not be an enum case | the kind is recoverable from the node |
| `law.add.open` | closed kinds may not define what the graph can represent | 15 cases decide it |

The deny table in `CLAUDE.md` §1 already carries BOTH rows by name —
`ANY uppercase identifier` and `companion *_kind/*_type` — and `audit100`
enforces them as `upper` and `companion`. **`NodeKind` fires both in one
identifier.**

`audit100` walks the canonical `.id` corpus. `src/*.zig` is the bootstrap
ledger and is excluded from every deny row. So the compiler — the artifact that
enforces the law on every other file — is the single corpus the law does not
reach.

`NodeKind` still enumerates **`module`, `directive`, `concept`, `pipeline`**.
`directive` was retired by §0.2 ("prefix `@` does not exist"), `concept` and
`pipeline` are pre-epoch-2. A closed enum outlived the semantics it enumerated,
which is what closed kinds do — and nothing convicted it because nothing looks.

## Why the bootstrap exemption does not cover this

`law.host.projection` is precise: the bootstrap may look un-duon-like **only
where the host language requires it**. That is the whole grant.

Zig does not require a companion `*Kind` enum. §0.3's canonical form —
`kind: { name, number, eof }` inline at the field — has a direct Zig spelling
(an anonymous enum field, or a tagged union whose tag is the node itself). The
enum is a DESIGN CHOICE that happens to be sheltering under a grant written for
things like pointer arithmetic and `extern` layout.

That is the general defect, and it is worth more than the 46 instances:
**"bootstrap ledger" has come to mean "unaudited", when the law says it means
"projected, named, and carrying a deletion gate."**

## The repair, in order

1. **A ratcheting scan over `src/*.zig`** for the rows the deny table already
   defines — `upper`, `companion`, and the ARCH-RELATION shapes (`addNode`,
   `addEdge`, `addChild`, `register*`, `emit*`). Ratchet DOWN. This is the item
   that makes the rest real; without it every repair is voluntary.
2. **Every host API names the relation it projects** (`law.host.projection`),
   carrying `authority = false`, its semantic owner, and its deletion gate. An
   API that cannot name its relation is the finding.
3. **`NodeKind`/`EdgeKind` demote to indexes**, not ontology — already ruled in
   C0 (`kinds.role = .index`, `kinds.authority = false`). Delete `directive`,
   `concept` and `pipeline` when the demotion lands; they name nothing.
4. `addChild` has no semantic existence (`law.fact.one`); `parent contains
   child` is the fact.

## The trap for whoever takes this

**Do not bulk-rename.** `duo-lawone-predicate-collisions` records that a
mechanical `is_*` rename collided on 25 of 71 sites, four of them against their
own constructors in the same file. A 791-row uppercase sweep across the
bootstrap compiler will be worse, and the compiler is the one artifact that
cannot be allowed to break.

Land the SCAN first, ratcheting from the measured baseline. Then repair by
subsystem with the differentials green at each step, which is `law.differential`
applied to the compiler's own source: old is oracle, new is candidate, the
differential judges.
