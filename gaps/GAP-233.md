# GAP-233 — one diagnostic identity is carrying four causes

**Status:** OPEN · **Filed:** !2026-08-25T06:20:00Z
**Priority:** P1 · **Kind:** diagnostic_identity
**Filed by:** claude-reconcile
**Files:** src/dnir_lower.zig,src/main.zig,gate/application.sh

## The measurement

`idol compile <subject> --backend=c --emit=c` over `examples/`, x86_64-linux-gnu.
Of 253 subjects, 210 pass `idol check`; of those, 141 refuse with
`missing-application-id` and 13 realize.

Until `5f74ee27` the C arm printed only the note, so all 141 reached a reader as
the same six words. `refuseApplication` had been binding `diagnostic.relation`
from `applicationFace` the whole time and the renderer discarded it — the arm
twenty lines below it, for `c_backend.emitSource`, already printed its own
`in relation:`. With the face printed, 141 separates into four:

    63  compiler intrinsic (`__*`)   __emit 10, __comptimematch 6, __c_call 5,
                                     __static_assert 4, __comptimemap 3, and
                                     ~20 more, each 1–2
    43  host relation, no entity     new 9, assert 5, setmetatable 4, type 2,
                                     cast 2, typeof, sqrt, random, …
    20  no face bound at all         the `invalidGraphFacts` path, which binds
                                     no occurrence
    15  non-callable subject applied tt 11, u, v4f64 3

60 distinct faces in total.

## Why this is a defect and not a report

**`law.fact.producer.one` in the diagnostic layer.** One identity that four
causes reach is not an identity; it is a bucket. A reader who greps
`missing-application-id` cannot tell which question was asked, and a gate that
pins the count cannot tell which one it caught — the same sentence
`a59c8ef9` used when it renamed `result-pack-arity` because two producers
spelled one note.

Each family wants a different answer:

- **compiler intrinsic** — `__emit`, `__c_call`, `__static_assert` and the
  `__comptime*` set are compile-time faces. Whether they should have relation
  entities at all is a question for that owner, and `GAP-226`'s acceptance does
  not cover them. They are the LARGEST family and were being read as that gap.
- **host relation with no entity** — this is `GAP-226` proper, and it is 43 of
  141, not 141.
- **no face bound** — the refusal reaches `invalidGraphFacts` with no occurrence
  to attribute. That is either a scan that found nothing to bind or a consumer
  refusing without one; the two are not distinguishable from the outside today.
- **non-callable subject applied** — `examples/call_index_module.id` is

      tt: [4]i64 = {1, 2, 3, 4}
      tt(2)

  which `law.md` §5 rules in one sentence ("`()` is ordinary application. It
  never means table indexing") and `gate/application.sh:333` already names as a
  NEGATIVE probe. It passes `idol check` and then refuses here as a missing id.
  A correct refusal wearing the wrong cause is worse than a wrong answer,
  because the ratchet that counts it will move when the wall is repaired and
  nothing about this subject changed.

## Three producers, one note — measured, not inferred

`grep -n '"missing-application-id"' src/dnir_lower.zig` returns three
non-test sites:

    169   invalidGraphFacts(ctx.diagnostic, site, …)   binds via
          `ctx.occurrences.get(expr)`, else falls back to `exprFace(expr)`
    2954  refuseApplication(…, application)            the bridge scan, binds
          the exact occurrence
    2955  invalidGraphFacts(diagnostic, @src(), …)     binds NOTHING

So the `no face bound` family is not one path failing to bind; it is the third
producer, which has no occurrence to bind, plus whatever reaches 169 with an
`exprFace` of null — a callee that is neither `.name` nor `.field`, such as an
application of an application.

This is the `law.fact.producer.one` sentence in its plainest form: three sites
mint one note, so the note identifies a site's opinion rather than a cause, and
no consumer downstream can tell them apart.

## Its effect on GAP-226's own ranking

The wall's site census ranks `host-relation-no-entity` +
`host-relation-name-declared-in-home` at 74% of unpublished applications. That
is a count of SITES. This is a count of SUBJECTS THAT CANNOT BE REALIZED, and
the ranking inverts: intrinsics 63, wall 43. Both are true and they answer
different questions — one program is refused by its first unresolved occurrence,
so a subject is attributed to whichever cause it meets first, not to the cause
with the most sites in it.

Neither number is wrong and neither is the other's substitute. Recording that
here because the two were about to be read as one.

## Acceptance

1. Each family refuses under its own note, minted where the cause is known —
   at minimum `non-callable-subject` separated from `missing-application-id`,
   since law already rules it and a gate already probes it.
2. `examples/call_index_module.id` and `examples/call_index_assign.id` refuse
   with the non-callable note, and `idol check` is the arm that decides whether
   they should have been accepted at all.
3. The `no face bound` family either binds one or says why it cannot, so that
   20 subjects stop reporting a cause nobody can act on.
4. The four-way split is recomputed by a runner rather than pinned here. The
   loop that produced it is in `gaps/GAP-226.md`; the face is now available in
   the refusal output, so classifying is `grep 'in relation:'`.
