# GAP-106 — the projection algebra is blocked at rung 0: a descriptor cannot be attached to a representation

**Status:** OPEN · **Filed:** 2026-08-09
| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Law:** `law.nominal` (§46) · REP-VIEW / REFINE / LIFT-SAFE (owner directive 2026-08-09) · VOCAB-ONE **Supersedes nothing; it is the precondition the whole projection design rests on.** |

## The design, stated so the blocker is legible

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | The owner directive asks for `str` to be, not "a slice of bytes", but: |

```
str
  representation → slice(byte)
  encoding       → utf8
  invariant      → valid(utf8)
```

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | and for operations to be INHERITED from the representation under a rule (**LIFT-SAFE**): `R:op → R'` lifts to `A:op → A'` iff `requires(op) ⊆ facts(A)` and `preserves(op) ⊇ invariants(A')`. |
| 2 | Otherwise the lift is refused, weakened, or checked. |
| 3 | That is what makes `slice` lift to `str` while `put(byte)` does not — one destroys `valid(utf8)`. |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **None of it is reachable, because rung 0 does not work.** |

```duo
feet: f64          -- declares
d: feet = 3.0      -- error: type mismatch: variable 'd' declared as 'feet'
```

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | A descriptor cannot be bound to a value of its own representation. gap[093] filed this and it is still open: three canonical spellings (`feet: f64`, `feet = f64`, `feet@f64`) all parse and all fail at the binding, and the only spelling that compiles is `type`, which §1 DENIES and which loses the value on the C backend. |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **So there is no representation to lift operations from.** REP-VIEW, REFINE, ERASE, LIFT-SAFE, SECTION-RETRACT and the rest are all downstream of a binding rule that does not exist. |

## The measured cost of not having it

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | `str` vs `string` today: |

```
lib/str.id       23 lines    2 relations:  sub, len
lib/string.id   361 lines   34 declarations, EVERY one named std_string_*
```

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | The 34: `starts_with` `ends_with` `contains` `index_of` `last_index_of` `count` `split` `split_n` `join` `lines` `replace` `replace_all` `repeat` `repeat_str` `reverse` `trim` `ltrim` `rtrim` `trim_prefix` `trim_suffix` `pad_left` `pad_right` `to_lower` `to_upper` `to_title_case` `capitalize` `char_at` `is_alpha` `is_digit` `is_space` `is_alphanumeric` `is_empty` … |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **`std_string_starts_with` encodes home + representation + operation + axis in one identifier, with three underscores.** That is VOCAB-ONE's exact prohibition: *standard idol names semantic relations, not implementation homes, representation categories, argument types, or historical subsystems.* |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | And the redundancy is total: reached ambiently the call is `std.string.std_string_starts_with(...)` — the home appears twice. |

## Why renaming these is the WRONG first move

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | The tempting repair is 34 renames: `std_string_starts_with` → `starts`. |
| 2 | It would satisfy LAW-ONE and change nothing that matters, because the defect is not spelling — **it is that these 34 are string-SPECIFIC implementations of operations that belong to sequences.** `starts`, `contains`, `find`, `count`, `split`, `join`, `reverse`, `pad` are relations on a sequence whose element happens to be a byte. |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | Renaming leaves 34 hand-written implementations that a second sequence descriptor would have to write again. |
| 2 | The owner's own acceptance test says so: |

> Adding a new sequence descriptor should automatically acquire most of the same
> vocabulary that `str` has, without implementing those functions again.

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A rename passes the idiom gate and fails that test.** It is cosmetic canonical migration — explicitly prohibited work. |

## The order this forces

1. **gap[093]: the nominal binding rule.** `d: feet = 3.0` must typecheck when
   `feet`'s representation is `f64`, and `d: feet = m` where `m: meters` must
   NOT. Without it there is no `str → slice(byte)` to speak of. This lives in
   sema.
2. **REP-VIEW + REFINE as facts**: a representation edge that preserves semantic
   identity, and a refinement that adds an invariant (`slice(byte)` +
   `valid(utf8)` → `str`).
3. **LIFT-SAFE**: the requires/preserves check. Only now can `slice` lift and
   `put(byte)` be refused.
4. **THEN the basis collapse.** Target basis from the directive:
   `at · len · slice · iter · find · eq · compare · concat · encode · decode`,
   with `starts`/`ends`/`contains`/`count`/`split`/`join`/`lines`/`trim`/
   `replace`/`reverse`/`map`/`fold`/`hash` DERIVED, and
   `codepoint`/`grapheme`/`normalize`/`case`/`collate` added as text-specific
   levels.

## The measurement that should gate it

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Standard basis size**: how many primitive relation families are required to derive the standard vocabulary. |
| 2 | Today `str` + `string` is 2 primitives and 34 hand-written helpers. |
| 3 | The target is ~10 primitives and 0 hand-written helpers. |
| 4 | That number is far stronger than LOC, and it is the honest scoreboard for "irreducible compactness". |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | Second gate, from the directive and worth automating: **add a new sequence descriptor and count how much vocabulary it acquires for free.** Today the answer is zero. |

## Do not

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Do not rename the 34.** It satisfies a grep and defeats the purpose. |

| # | directive |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Do not add `starts`/`contains` to `str.id` as new native primitives.** That grows the basis in the wrong direction and makes the eventual derivation harder, because a hand-written primitive is a thing the lifting law must later be shown not to contradict. |
