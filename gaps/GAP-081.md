# GAP-081 — canonical orientation is 1647 to 1 against, and half of it cannot compile

**Status:** OPEN · **Filed:** 2026-08-08
**Law:** Pass 121 Amendment §1 FACE-SUBJECT · §9 (`to` is the archetypal
orientation fixture) · §16 IDIOM-TOTAL · §17 (canonicalization performs the
repair) · §31 (canonical source is a fixed point)

## The measurement

Pass 121 §9 makes the orientation law concrete: `n:to(str)` is canonical,
`to(str)(n)` is not. Measured over tracked `.duo`:

| form | sites | files |
|---|---:|---:|
| `to(x)(y)` — operation-first immediate conversion | **1647** | 250 |
| `y:to(x)` — canonical subject-first | **1** | 1 |

The canonical form appears **once in the entire tree**. This is not drift at the
margin; the corpus is written in the noncanonical orientation essentially
without exception, and the one canonical site is the exception.

## Before rewriting anything: does the canonical form work?

Half of it does. Measured by VALUE, not by inspection — which is the whole point
of checking first, because a canonicalizer that rewrites 1647 sites into a form
the compiler cannot lower would take the tree down.

| conversion | operation-first | subject-first |
|---|---|---|
| `i64 -> str` | `42` | `42` |
| `f64 -> str` | — | `2.5` |
| `str -> i64` (typed `s: str`) | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |

`to(str)` canonicalizes cleanly in **both** faces. `to(i64)` fails in **both**
faces with `error: call to undeclared function 'lua_to_num'`, so it is NOT an
orientation defect — it is a missing conversion, and orientation is innocent.

One further asymmetry, worth recording because it will mislead someone:
`to(i64)(std.proc.capture(cmd))` — operation-first over an *untyped call result*
— compiles and runs, while the subject-first spelling of the same expression
boxes into `lua_invoke` and fails to build. So the two faces are not
interchangeable today even where one of them works.

## What this means for the canonicalizer

Pass 121 §17 says canonicalization performs the repair mechanically. It can, for
part of the corpus, and must decline the rest:

- **`to(str)` sites: REWRITE.** Both faces lower identically; the rewrite is
  semantics-preserving and provable by value.
- **`to(i64)` sites: DO NOT TOUCH** until `lua_to_num` exists. Rewriting them
  changes a program that fails to build into a different program that fails to
  build, which is churn that looks like progress.
- Every rewrite emits the §18 witness (relation, subject, level, graph identity
  unchanged) so the change is justified rather than trusted.

## The honest reading of 1647:1

It is tempting to read this as 250 files' worth of bad taste. It is at least
partly **capability**: the canonical spelling of the most common conversion
direction in this tree (`str -> i64`, every parse of a number) does not compile
in either face. Authors wrote what worked.

That is the sharper finding, and it inverts the repair order. **Fix the
conversion before enforcing the orientation** — otherwise the gate demands a
form the compiler cannot produce, and the first agent to hit it will conclude
the law is advisory.

## Cited site, left noncanonical on purpose

`scripts/bootstrap_scan.duo` keeps `to(i64)(std.proc.capture(cmd))` with a
comment naming this gap. It is the only spelling that builds. Converting it
would be the exact mistake this gap exists to prevent.

## Fix, in order

1. `lua_to_num` — make `str -> i64` build in both faces. Fixture asserts the
   VALUE (`"17":to(i64) == 17`), both backends, since a conversion that compiles
   and answers 0 is the failure mode `tonumber` already has in this tree.
2. A `convert` row in `audit100` counting operation-first immediate conversions,
   ratcheting DOWN from 1647.
3. The canonicalizer rewrite for `to(str)`, witnessed per §18.
4. Only then the `to(i64)` sites.
