# GAP-200 — single-assignment synchronization

**Status:** OPEN · **Filed:** !2026-08-18T17:05:00Z
**Priority:** P1 · **Kind:** research_gap
**Filed by:** devin-semantic-20260818
**Files:** docs/spec/constitution.md, docs/spec/law.md, src/semantic_graph.zig

A semantic identity can be demanded before its value is determined. Consumers
block/depend on the identity. When it becomes determined, dependent computation
proceeds. This unifies futures, promises, dataflow variables, and single-
assignment synchronization as one semantic determinacy fact.

## Current measured boundary

`async` and `yield` are mentioned but not modeled. There is no `determinacy` fact.

**Crossed:**

- `id` and `demand` are graph facts.
- `law.change.delta` and `law.world` exist.

**Still missing:**

- `determinacy(id)` = `unknown` | `determined`.
- Consumer edges that depend on an unresolved identity.
- Wakeup/propagation when determinacy changes.
- Unification with futures/promises: `await x` becomes dependency on `x`.
- No `Future<T>` or `Promise<T>` objects required.
- No `sync` or `dataflow` subsystem: determinacy is a fact on `id`, consumer-wait is a demand edge, and wakeup is a scheduler behavior.

## Required order

1. Add `determinacy` as a graph fact on every semantic identity.
2. Add consumer-wait edges that are resolved when determinacy becomes known.
3. Implement propagation: a determined value triggers its dependents.
4. Derive `Future`, `Promise`, `async`, `await` from the same algebra.
5. Preserve determinism where the graph proves order is not observable.

## Closure acceptance

Close when `await x` and `x` dependency on a dataflow variable are the same
mechanism, and no `Future`/`Promise` object is needed in source.
