# GAP-105 — SHC measured by AUTHORITY, not by proof: one stage of seventeen is live

**Status:** OPEN · **Filed:** 2026-08-09
**Law:** §0b THE DESCENT IS DUO TO THE BYTE · G9 the bootstrap ledger is a
licensed exception WITH A TERMINATION CONDITION
**Answers:** *"prioritize getting to 100% shc no zig by any means necessary"* —
by naming what the remaining distance actually is.

## The measurement

```
Zig compiler          125368 lines   114 files   src/*.zig
duon compiler           5333 lines    17 modules  lib/std/compiler/*.duo
generated C linked into production    2 files: duo_lexer_tokenize.c,
                                               duo_keyword_classify.c (deleted)
```

**Seventeen stages exist in duon. ONE is authoritative.** `tokenizeAuthority()`
returns `.duo_native`; nothing else has an authority function at all —
`grep -oE "[a-zA-Z]+Authority\(\)" src/*.zig` finds only `tokenizeAuthority` and
`keywordAuthority`, both in `duo_lexer_bridge.zig`.

## Why "~60% self-hosting" overstates it

The external audit puts self-hosting at ~60%, and by MILESTONE coverage that is
fair: SH-03 (lexer) through SH-11 (ARM64 encoder) each have a proof in
`examples/pass16_*_proof.duo` and a module in `lib/std/compiler/`. Those proofs
are real and they pass.

But a proof is not an authority. Measured by what the shipping compiler actually
RUNS, self-hosting is **one stage** — the lexer — and `src/lexer.zig` survives
only as a class-C differential reference. By lines under duon authority the
figure is closer to **957 / 125368**.

**Both numbers are true and they answer different questions.** "Can duon express
this stage?" is ~60% and rising. "Does the compiler run on duon?" is one stage.
The second is what "100% SHC no zig" asks for, and it is the one nothing
currently reports.

## The mechanism is proven and repeatable — that is the good news

SH-03 established the whole pattern, end to end, and it is five steps:

1. the stage exists in duon (`lib/std/compiler/lexer.duo`)
2. it is compiled to C through `lib/std/compiler/host.duo` and the artifact is
   TRACKED (`src/duo_lexer_tokenize.c`), so the compiler links a duon-authored
   stage without needing a duon compiler to already exist
3. a differential runs the duon stage against the Zig one field-for-field on
   every test run (`src/duo_lexer_dispatch.zig`)
4. an authority function flips (`tokenizeAuthority() -> .duo_native`)
5. the Zig original is reclassified C (reference-only) and deleted at S1 closure

**Nothing about this is lexer-specific.** It is the general procedure for
retiring any stage, and it has been executed exactly once.

written, it sits directly behind the one stage that is live, and every later
stage consumes its output — so flipping it converts the most downstream Zig from
"authoritative" to "differential reference" in one move.

## What is owed before that flip

The five-step pattern needs steps 2 and 3 for the parser, and neither exists:

- **no generated artifact.** `src/*.c` holds only the lexer's. The parser needs
  the same `host.duo` treatment, tracked, with the regeneration command
  documented beside it (CLAUDE.md already carries the lexer's, and warns that
  editing `lexer.duo` alone does nothing).
- **no differential.** `duo_lexer_dispatch.zig` compares token streams field for
  field. The parser's equivalent compares AST shape, and it is the harder half —
  it must convict a divergence rather than merely notice a crash.

**Do not flip an authority without step 3.** SH-03's differential is what makes
its flip trustworthy; without one, an authority switch is a claim.

## Do not

**Do not count proofs as self-hosting.** `examples/pass16_*_proof.duo` prove a
duon stage AGREES with the Zig one on fixtures. That is step 3's ancestor, not
step 4. Seventeen passing proofs and one live stage is the actual position, and
a plan sized on "~60%" will be sized wrong by an order of magnitude.

**Do not delete Zig ahead of an authority flip.** G9 makes `src/*.zig` a
licensed exception with a termination condition; the condition is the flip, not
the existence of a duon module beside it.
