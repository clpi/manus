# GAP-107 — the `@` that should die works; the `@` that should live does not

**Status:** OPEN (measured) · **Filed:** 2026-08-09
**Law:** AT-ONE · PREFIX-ZERO · AMBIENT-DYAD · ANCHOR (owner directive
2026-08-09) · §0.2 · `docs/directive_erasure.md`

## The ruling being measured against

The owner froze four laws:

- **AT-ONE** — `@` only denotes or establishes a semantic anchor; never
  "compiler magic".
- **PREFIX-ZERO** — prefix directive syntax does not exist.
- **AMBIENT-DYAD** — `@` supplies the ambient descriptor, `.` the ambient
  subject.
- **ANCHOR** — `x@r` selects relation `r` anchored at `x`.

And a specific reading of `@{…}`: it is **compositional**, `@` + `{…}`, meaning
*apply the enclosing descriptor to this pack*. It is NOT its own
descriptor-literal syntax. That makes it APPLY-ONE plus ambient recovery, and
gives `@` zero special construction semantics.

## Measured 2026-08-09 — the inversion

**Neither surviving form works:**

```duo
origin = () @{ 0.0, 0.0 }
  ->  error: expected expression

r = point@ordering
  ->  error: use of undeclared identifier 'point'
```

`@{…}` does not parse in expression position at all. `x@rel` parses far enough
to resolve `point`, then fails because a descriptor is not reachable as a value
there — the same dynamic-member/binding family as gap[093] and gap[100] rung 4.

**Meanwhile the legacy prefix surface is alive and load-bearing:**

```
105  @ffi
100  @c.emit
 27  @hot
 16  @comp.c.export
 12  @location
 11  @c.export
  9  @run
  7  @comp.each
```

`@c.emit` is not merely accepted — it is how `src/duo_lexer_tokenize.c` is
generated, the artifact the compiler links and tokenizes itself with, and it is
the mechanism behind SH-03, the one self-hosted stage. `lib/std/os.duo` alone
has 8 sites.

**So the `@` PREFIX-ZERO abolishes is the only `@` that functions, and the `@`
AT-ONE reserves the sigil for is unimplemented.**

## Why this is not just "another unimplemented form"

CLAUDE.md already warns that `@` had two contradictory homes and that "agents
cited whichever half suited the diff". The four new laws resolve the AMBIGUITY
cleanly — one irreducible job, semantic anchoring — but resolving it on paper
inverts the implementation's actual shape rather than describing it.

That matters for sequencing. **PREFIX-ZERO cannot be enforced by deleting `@`
spellings**, because deleting `@c.emit` deletes the self-hosted lexer. The
erasure is gated on the anchoring forms working AND on the C-emission path
having a duon-native replacement — which is gap[105]'s SHC spine, not a lexical
cleanup.

## What each law needs before it is load-bearing

| law | blocker |
|---|---|
| **AMBIENT-DYAD** (`@`) | `@{…}` must parse compositionally in expression position. Today: `expected expression`. |
| **AMBIENT-DYAD** (`.`) | the ambient subject does not parse either — `. * 2.0` gives `ExpectedToken` (recorded in gap[100]). **Both halves of the dyad are unimplemented.** |
| **ANCHOR** (`x@r`) | a descriptor must be reachable as a value at the anchor position. Same family as gap[093]. |
| **AT-ONE** | nothing to enforce until the above exist; enforcing it now would only delete working machinery. |
| **PREFIX-ZERO** | gated on the SHC spine replacing `@c.emit`'s role, not on a grep. |

## The honest reading

`@` is currently **100% bootstrap debt and 0% semantic anchoring** — the exact
inverse of the intended end state. The laws are right; the distance is larger
than a spelling migration, and the census above is the measurement of it.

## Do not

**Do not lower the `@` count to satisfy PREFIX-ZERO.** CLAUDE.md already states
this ("do not delete existing ones to lower a grep count") and the reason is now
concrete: 100 of the 105 highest-count sites are `@ffi`/`@c.emit`, and the
second is the self-hosting seed.

**Do not implement `@{…}` as descriptor-literal syntax** to make it parse. The
owner's reading is explicit — compositional, `@` + `{…}`, APPLY-ONE plus ambient
recovery. A dedicated literal form would give `@` construction semantics the
ruling exists to deny, and would be a new syntax where the directive says none.
